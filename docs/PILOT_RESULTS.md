# Pilot results and interpretation

## Provenance
Generated by `python -m src.experiment --out experiments/results --seeds 11 22 33 --students 500 --questions-per-topic 15 --epochs 2`. Results are computed on the bundled toy generator and a standard-library logistic regression implementation; no external ML package, pretrained language model, real question bank, or learner dataset was used. The raw per-seed JSON is `experiments/results/seed_results.json`; aggregate values are in `experiments/results/summary.csv`.

## What these figures do and do not mean

- EWC has the numerically highest mean accuracy in this pilot; differences are small and there are only three seeds. No inferential significance claim is made.
- BWT is a difference score; values near zero indicate little measured change under this setup, not general resistance to forgetting. Task-isolated models obtain zero by construction and use separate predictors per task.
- FWT compares post-prior-task predictions on future tasks with a zero-initialized model. Its relatively high values arise from the generic linear features and the synthetic generator; it is not evidence of transfer in learners or multilingual encoders.
- “Cumulative joint” is trained on all topics seen so far at each stage; it is not a full-data oracle trained once on the complete dataset.
- Adaptation-by-history values in raw JSON are exploratory only. They are not an adaptation-speed metric and are excluded from the manuscript because difficulty/order effects are confounded with history length.
- The 45,000 rows are not 45,000 independent participants; there are 500 simulated profiles, and performance is conditional on a toy data-generating process.
- The experiment does not evaluate Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, transformers, PNN, a student-specific adapter, live online updates, or educational efficacy.

For a submission-grade study, run a substantially broader preregistered experiment with multiple task orders, sensitivity/tuning on validation data, verified item content, language-review gates, appropriately powered seeds and uncertainty analysis, and then a real-data evaluation if efficacy claims are intended.
